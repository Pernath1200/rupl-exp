# Level audit — 2026-08-07

James asked whether the level structure is on the right track. Measured, not
estimated. **Headline: the grammar spine is sound; the vocabulary volume is not,
and it fails in a specific, structural way that is worth fixing deliberately.**

## 2. Vocabulary delivered vs CEFR expectation

Curated deck items (the words the course actually *teaches* as vocabulary,
counted across every block of every vocab pack):

| level | new items | cumulative | typical CEFR receptive target | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 677 | 677 | ~500 | **ahead** |
| A2 | 341 | 1,018 | ~1,000–1,500 | about right |

Distinct Polish word-forms across all vocab decks: **1,126**.

The separate figure of ~2,600 "taught forms" in the audit tooling is *inflected
forms*, not words — `kupię`/`kupiłem`/`kupował` are three forms of one verb. It
measures grammar coverage, not vocabulary size, and must not be quoted as a
vocabulary count.

## 3. What this means

- **A1 and A2 are genuinely at level.** Vocabulary and grammar both land where
  the labels claim.
- **B1 upward, the course is a grammar syllabus with a vocabulary rump.** By C1
  it adds 24 new words across the whole level. A learner finishing RUPL as it
  stands would have C1-level *structural* control and roughly B1 *lexical*
  range — able to inflect almost anything, but not to read a newspaper.
- **The curve runs backwards.** Vocabulary need grows fastest at the upper
  levels; RUPL's vocabulary supply shrinks fastest there (677 → 341 → 84 → 36 →
  24).

## 4. Why it happened — a real cause, not an oversight

The **vocab register principle** (B1-SPINE, carried into B2 and C1) says upper
levels must not revisit A1/A2 topic domains and should add *registers* instead.
It was written to stop duplication, and at that job it worked. But paired with
spines that treat B2 and C1 as **fence-payoff projects** — closing every
grammatical debt the earlier levels logged — it left nobody responsible for
lexical volume. C1-SPINE's O8 states the assumption out loud: *"after four
levels there is genuinely less register left than there was at B2."* That is
true of *registers* and false of *words*.

## 5. What is NOT wrong

- **Sequencing.** 0 audit errors across 199 nodes; nothing is used before it is
  taught, at any level.
- **Grammar levelling.** Participles, the conditional past, verbal nouns,
  complex subordination and the plural adjective genuinely belong at B2/C1.
  Nothing obviously sits at the wrong level.
- **B1's lightness is partly real, partly cosmetic.** 29 units, but they carry
  the aspect system, the conditional, prefixed motion, the full dative and
  relative clauses — dense units, not padding. It could absorb more vocabulary
  comfortably.

## 6. Options, for James

Not started — this is his call, and his standing instruction is that nothing
expands until what exists is tested.

1. **Do nothing yet.** Finish C1's grammar, smoke the course, decide later.
   The framework claim stands; the C1 *label* is the only thing overstated.
2. **Re-label.** Keep the content, call the top level B2. Honest immediately,
   costs nothing, and the grammar genuinely reaches C1 even if the lexicon does
   not.
3. **Build a vocabulary spine for B1–C1.** The real fix, and the largest job:
   roughly 1,500–2,000 more curated items to reach a defensible B2, several
   thousand for C1. Would need a different authoring shape from the current
   12-items-per-pack decks — frequency-list driven, thematically broad, and
   probably reading-passage based rather than word-list based.
4. **Split the claim.** State plainly in the app that RUPL teaches grammar to
   C1 and vocabulary to B1, and point at extensive reading for the rest. This
   is the most honest description of what the course currently is, and it
   matches how James already describes it: *"a framework, which is what I
   wanted."*
